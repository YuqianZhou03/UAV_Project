\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\noindent Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) swarms are increasingly used in surveillance, emergency response, and environmental monitoring, but their distributed and resource-constrained nature makes them vulnerable to cyber attacks such as spoofing, jamming, and coordinated communication abuse. Centralized intrusion detection is often unsuitable in this context because it requires raw data sharing, stable connectivity, and high communication bandwidth, which may conflict with mission constraints and privacy regulations. This thesis presents a federated intrusion detection mechanism with adaptive privacy for UAV swarms, combining a Federated Learning (FL) IDS pipeline, a Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based trust model, and an Adaptive Privacy Controller (APC). In the proposed framework, each UAV trains locally and shares model updates instead of raw telemetry; the RL module evaluates participant reliability and supports robust collaboration under heterogeneous and partially unreliable clients; and the APC dynamically adjusts privacy-related parameters, including participation intensity, update cadence, and perturbation level, according to real-time threat level, mission criticality, trust status, and available resources. The integrated design targets a practical balance among detection effectiveness, privacy protection, and operational efficiency. The overall implementation and evaluation demonstrate that combining FL, trust-aware coordination, and context-aware privacy adaptation provides a feasible and scalable security architecture for next-generation UAV swarm networks.

\vspace{0.75em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} UAV swarms; intrusion detection; federated learning; reinforcement learning; adaptive privacy controller.

\section{Division of responsibilities}
The project was organized around four tightly coupled technical streams that map to the core chapters of this thesis. Responsibilities were assigned by subsystem ownership, while integration, experiment execution, and documentation were carried out collaboratively.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Li Yan (Federated Learning stream):} Led the FL backbone design and implementation, including data split orchestration and client-round mapping (\texttt{swarm\_fl\_data.py}), federated training workflow (\texttt{server.py}, \texttt{client.py}), and reproducible model checkpoint/evaluation pipeline. This stream established the end-to-end training loop used by all other modules.

  \item \textbf{Zhou Yuqian (IDS stream):} Led IDS model and data pipeline work, including UAV traffic preprocessing and feature-window construction (\texttt{preprocess\_swarm\_federated.py}, \texttt{enrich\_swarm\_telemetry.py}), CNN--LSTM IDS modeling (\texttt{model.py}), and operational visualization support via the dashboard and workflow simulator (\texttt{dashboard/}, \texttt{uav\_workflow\_sim.py}).

  \item \textbf{Liu Guixin (RL trust stream):} Led the trust-aware RL module design and integration, including TrustNet modeling, replay-based update logic, state/reward definitions, and the FL trust bridge for per-client trust scoring (\texttt{RL\_module/core.py}, \texttt{RL\_module/RL.py}). This stream provided adaptive trust signals for robust aggregation and policy context.

  \item \textbf{Zhang Wenyue (Adaptive Privacy Controller stream):} Led APC design and implementation, including context modeling, rule-based participation/frequency/noise control, and YAML-configurable policy logic (\texttt{apc\_module/apc\_core.py}, \texttt{apc\_module/rules.py}, \texttt{apc\_module/models.py}). This stream translated mission and threat context into actionable privacy controls.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Coordination and integration process.}  
The team followed a weekly integration cycle: (i) subsystem-level coding and unit checks; (ii) cross-module interface alignment around shared metrics (threat, trust, resource, and privacy parameters); (iii) joint end-to-end FL runs and ablation execution (\texttt{run\_rl\_ablation.py}); and (iv) synchronized analysis and thesis writing. Final results, figures, and conclusions were validated jointly, while each member remained the primary author of their corresponding technical chapter.

\section{Cross-module Integration and Orchestration}
This section describes how the four technical modules are integrated into a closed-loop UAV swarm defense system in operational rounds. The implementation is not a loose coupling of independent scripts; instead, it is a round-based orchestration where Federated Learning (FL), IDS inference, RL trust modeling, and APC privacy control exchange structured signals at each cycle.

At the start of each FL round, the server broadcasts per-client training policy through the fit-configuration stage in \texttt{server.py}. The policy includes participation flag, noise scale, update frequency, and privacy accounting fields (epsilon/delta/clip norm). On each UAV client in \texttt{client.py}, local IDS training is executed on the assigned shard. If APC policy disables participation or enforces frequency skipping, the client returns global weights directly; otherwise, it performs local optimization, clips weight deltas, and applies Gaussian perturbation to updates before uplink.

After receiving client updates, the server performs trust-aware aggregation in the \texttt{RLAPCFedAvg} strategy. Effective contribution weight is computed from sample size, trust score, resource factor, and anomaly factor, which reduces the influence of suspicious or unstable clients while preserving useful contributions from reliable nodes. In parallel, centralized evaluation computes accuracy, loss, and macro precision/recall, and these metrics are transformed into global and per-client threat indicators.

The RL bridge in \texttt{RL\_module/core.py} converts per-round observations into trust scores using replay-based temporal updates, and APC in \texttt{apc\_module/apc\_core.py} converts threat, mission criticality, trust, and resource context into next-round policy for each UAV. These policies are then fed back to the next round through server-side pending configuration, completing a closed decision loop. Runtime telemetry and policy outputs are serialized into live dashboard records by \texttt{dashboard\_live.py}, enabling synchronized experiment tracing and ablation comparison.
